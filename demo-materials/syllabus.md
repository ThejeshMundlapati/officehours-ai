# CS 2100: Data Structures (Demo) — Syllabus

> This is a **fictional demo course** created to exercise the OfficeHours app.
> No real institution, course, or person is described here.

## Course overview

CS 2100 (Demo) covers the core data structures every programmer relies on:
arrays and dynamic arrays, linked lists, stacks and queues, recursion, binary
trees, binary search trees, heaps, hash tables, and an introduction to graphs.
Assignments are in Python 3.12.

- **Instructor:** Prof. Ada Placeholder
- **Teaching assistants:** the course TA team (see the OfficeHours app)
- **Lectures:** Mondays and Wednesdays, 10:00–11:30, Demo Hall 101

## Office hours policy

Office hours run through the **OfficeHours queue app**, not by email.

- TA office hours: Monday–Thursday, 2:00–6:00 PM, and Sunday 6:00–9:00 PM.
- Instructor office hours: Wednesday 1:00–2:30 PM.
- Join the queue with **one** question at a time. Write a clear title and say
  what you have already tried — the TA will read it before reaching you.
- Each visit is capped at **10 minutes** when more than five students are
  waiting, so everyone gets a turn. You may rejoin the queue afterward.
- TAs will help you debug and understand concepts, but they will **not** write
  your code or tell you whether a submission will earn full marks.
- If you leave the queue or do not respond within 3 minutes of being called,
  your question is closed and you may rejoin at the back.
- Office hours end at the posted time even if the queue is not empty. The queue
  closes for new questions 15 minutes before the end of each session.

## Late policy

- Every student has **4 late days** for the semester, usable on homework only.
- A late day extends a deadline by 24 hours. At most **2 late days** may be
  used on any single assignment.
- Late days are applied automatically, in whole days. Submitting 1 minute late
  uses one late day.
- Once your late days are used up, late homework loses **20% per day** and is
  not accepted more than 2 days after the deadline.
- Quizzes and exams cannot be taken late except with a documented emergency
  arranged in advance with the instructor.

## Exam dates

- **Midterm exam:** Wednesday, October 14, in lecture (75 minutes). Covers
  weeks 1–6: arrays, linked lists, stacks, queues, recursion, and Big-O.
- **Final exam:** Thursday, December 10, 1:00–3:00 PM, Demo Hall 101.
  Cumulative, with emphasis on trees, heaps, hash tables, and graphs.
- Both exams are closed-book. You may bring **one double-sided handwritten
  page** of notes.

## Academic integrity

You may discuss ideas with classmates, but all code you submit must be written
by you. Do not share solution code or post it publicly. If you use outside
resources, cite them in a comment at the top of your file.
